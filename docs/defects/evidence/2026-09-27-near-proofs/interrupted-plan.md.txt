## Context

The [near-decoder repair](../investigations/2026-09-27-near-y-decoder.md) made `canFoldNearIndex` (`MOSLegalizerInfo.cpp`) refuse to fold a near `G_PTR_ADD` into native 65816 indexed addressing unless the add is proven not to wrap at 16 bits:

- `NoUWrap` on the `G_PTR_ADD`, or
- `NoUSWrap` with a known-nonnegative offset, or
- known bits proving that `max(base) + max(offset)` fits in 16 bits.

Otherwise the full near address is materialized before the access. The guard is required: `abs,X`, `abs,Y` and `(dp),Y` carry out of the 16-bit address into `DBR+1`, while a near pointer wraps within the bank ([bank-wrap record](../defects/mos-near-index-bank-wrap.json)).

The report notes that "some loop-generated near pointer additions lack overflow flags even when their original IR had them". Nobody has measured the cost or found where the flags go.

### First trace (before measuring)

On the retained `far_rt_blit` test (`llvm/test/CodeGen/MOS/far-indir-indexed.ll`), `llc -print-after-all` shows the flags survive until **Loop Strength Reduction**:

```
before LSR: %d = getelementptr inbounds i8, ptr %dst, i8 %j
after LSR:  %0 = zext nneg i8 %j to i16
            %scevgep = getelementptr i8, ptr %dst, i16 %0
```

ScalarEvolution itself still knows the address is `{%dst,+,1}<nuw><%loop>`. LSR rebuilds the address from its formula registers with `getAddExpr(Ops)` and no flags, so `SCEVExpander::expandAddToGEP` emits a flag-free GEP. The IRTranslator faithfully copies GEP flags to `G_PTR_ADD`, so it has nothing to copy.

This is one example. The measurement below establishes whether it is the common cause.

## Approach

### Phase 1 — measure (decide GO/NO-GO)

A measurement-only build adds two hidden options to `canFoldNearIndex`. They are not committed:

- `-mos-near-index-trace` prints each verdict: function, base and offset defining opcodes, flags.
- `-mos-near-index-unsafe-fold-all` folds every refused add. This restores the pre-guard codegen and is **unsound**. It exists only to bound the opportunity.

`dev/measure-near-index-proofs.py` replays the retained 409-source census (`default`/`a16`/`xy16` × `Os`/`Oz`) per arm and records per-function sizes and trace verdicts.

- The bound is `guard` against `fold-all`. If the bound is negligible (under ~0.1% of census `.text` and no hot-loop function over a few bytes), stop and report NO-GO with the numbers.
- Attribute refusals to a pass. Compare trace counts with `-mllvm -disable-lsr`, and check the post-LSR IR of the largest contributors.

### Phase 2 — fix at the causal layer

Candidate designs:

1. **MOS IR pass after LSR (chosen unless measurement says otherwise).** A 65816-only function pass runs in `MOSPassConfig::addIRPasses` after the generic IR passes, so it runs after LSR. It re-derives `nuw` on near GEPs from ScalarEvolution. The proof obligation is exact: for a 16-bit base `B` and any 16-bit unsigned offset `O`, `B + O` does not wrap iff `(B + O) mod 2^16 >= B`. The pass sets `nuw` only when SCEV proves `GEP uge base`. SCEV's no-wrap facts come from the original IR flags (`inbounds`/`nuw`), and SCEV only trusts them where poison would be UB. That is the same reasoning IndVars uses to strengthen flags. A negative LSR offset (`dst + 0xFFFC`, the bank-wrap defect) yields `GEP < base` and stays refused.
2. *Rejected:* patching LSR or `SCEVExpander` generically to carry flags into rebuilt formulas. Every target would see the change. Soundness depends on each formula's decomposition, and it would be a larger generic patch against a heavily-tuned pass for a MOS-only payoff.
3. *Rejected:* weakening `canFoldNearIndex`, for example by trusting `inbounds` without a nonnegative offset. That reintroduces the bank-wrap miscompile.

Conservatism: the pass only **adds** `nuw` where SCEV proves it. Where SCEV cannot prove it, the pass leaves the GEP unchanged, which only misses a win. It does not touch far (`addrspace(2)`) or zero-page (`addrspace(1)`) GEPs, or non-65816 subtargets. A hidden `-mos-recover-near-nowrap` option (default on) enables A/B measurement.

### Patch placement

The pass is a MOS-target change, so its natural home is `0002` (all `llvm/lib/Target/MOS` codegen for #321, regenerated by `dev/regen-patch.sh`). A separate numbered patch would only be warranted if it were upstream-bound on its own. It is not: it serves the downstream-only near-index guard that also lives in `0002`.
